ことのね: Linking the Korean you know by its hanja roots.

2026-10-21 〜In developmentWeb (PWA)

You type a Sino-Korean word you've learned, in hangul, with your own fingers. It splits into its hanja roots (학 | 學 | 배울 학 | gaku, manabu), links to words you collected before, and shows words you can now read from the characters you have. Collect 학생 (學生) and 대학 (大學), and 대학생 (大學生), which you never studied, is already readable.

Why I made it

More than half the words in a Korean dictionary are Sino-Korean, and their characters are almost the same as in Japanese. Korean also reads each character with almost a single sound (學 is 학 in every word). So knowing one character's sound gives you the sound of every word it appears in, and learning turns from addition into multiplication.

What went into it

  • The hanja of words you haven't collected stay hidden as ？？. When you type the hangul yourself to collect a word, its characters open as the answer. Nothing is collected with a single tap.
  • Each word you collect shows, in large type, how many more words you can now read. So there are no stars or rarity: more strokes don't make a character worth more. The earlier version, 한자 도감, was about filling a field guide; here collecting is the result, not the goal.
  • The dictionary, about 21,200 words and 3,400 characters, is built from open data (kengdic, Unihan, the Basic Korean Dictionary, JMdict), with Japanese meanings for about 90% of the words. It runs entirely on the device, with no account.
  • In Meet in sentences, Kotonoame's example sentences fall and you meet your collected words again inside them. Where Kotonoame lines up sentences by their pattern, Kotonone lines up words by their characters.
